Given a toxicology guideline endpoint description (the Query), judge whether the ontology candidate (the Document) represents a phenotype that would be detected, scored, or measured by that endpoint.

CRITICAL RULES FOR SELECTION:

1. RELEVANCE OF SPECIFIC OUTCOMES: If the endpoint describes a general measurement (e.g., "liver weight", "body temperature", "clinical signs"), you MUST ACCEPT candidate phenotypes that represent specific outcomes of that measurement (e.g., "increased liver weight", "decreased body temperature", "abnormal behavior"). Specific directional or severity-based findings are valid targets of the general endpoint.

2. BIOLOGICAL ALIGNMENT: The candidate must biologically align with the tissue, organ, or process being targeted by the endpoint. Do not accept phenotypes of organs that are not examined by the endpoint.

3. MEASUREMENT TYPE ALIGNMENT: Ensure the type of measurement matches. A "weight" endpoint detects weight/mass phenotypes. A "histopathology" endpoint detects microscopic lesions or morphology changes. A "clinical chemistry" endpoint detects serum/blood biomarker levels.

Match them based strictly on whether observing or measuring the endpoint (Query) could plausibly lead to recording the candidate phenotype (Document).
