## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes a stepwise procedure for assessing acute oral toxicity using a minimum number of animals (typically 3 per step). It is designed to rank substances according to the Globally Harmonised System (GHS) classification categories based on fixed LD50 cut-off values rather than calculating a precise LD50. The method is intended to replace conventional LD50 tests where possible to reduce animal usage while maintaining reliability for hazard classification.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Individual observation at least once during the first 30 minutes after dosing.
- Periodic observations during the first 24 hours (special attention in the first 4 hours).
- Daily observations thereafter for up to 14 days.
- Specific signs monitored: changes in skin/fur, eyes/mucous membranes, respiratory/circulatory/autonomic/CNS systems, somatomotor activity, behavior patterns.
- Specific toxic signs: tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, coma.
- Recording of time of onset and duration of toxic effects.

### Haematology
- *Not required.* This is an acute study focused on mortality and gross pathology; haematology is not listed as a standard endpoint.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- *Not required.* Not listed as a standard endpoint for this acute classification method.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** All test animals (including those dying during the test or killed for humane reasons) must undergo gross necropsy. All gross pathological changes must be recorded.
- **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of organs showing evidence of gross pathology in animals surviving 24 or more hours may be considered (optional/recommended for useful information), but is not mandatory for all organs.
