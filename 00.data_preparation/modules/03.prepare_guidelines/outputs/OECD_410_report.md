## Purpose and Scope

This guideline provides information on possible health hazards likely to arise from repeated exposures by the dermal route over a limited period of time (21 or 28 days). It is used after initial acute toxicity information is obtained to determine subchronic dermal toxicity. The main difference between the two durations covered is the time over which dosing takes place.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Daily careful clinical examination for signs of toxicity (onset, degree, duration).
- Cage-side observations including changes in skin, fur, eyes, mucous membranes.
- Respiratory, circulatory, autonomic, and central nervous system effects.
- Somatomotor activity and behaviour pattern.
- Weekly measurements of food consumption.
- Weekly body weight measurements.

### Haematology
- Haematocrit
- Haemoglobin concentration
- Erythrocyte count
- Total and differential leucocyte count
- Measure of clotting potential (clotting time, prothrombin time, thromboplastin time, or platelet count)

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Calcium, Phosphorus, Chloride, Sodium, Potassium
- Fasting glucose
- Serum glutamic-pyruvic transaminase (ALT)
- Serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase (AST)
- Ornithine decarboxylase
- Gamma glutamyl transpeptidase
- Urea nitrogen
- Albumen
- Blood creatinine
- Total bilirubin
- Total serum protein
- *Optional/Additional:* Lipids, hormones, acid/base balance, methaemoglobin, cholinesterase activity.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Full examination of external surface, all orifices, cranial/thoracic/abdominal cavities.
- **Organ Weights (Wet):** Liver, kidneys, adrenals, testes (must be weighed as soon as possible).
- **Histopathology:** Examination of preserved organs/tissues of high dose group and control group. Target organs showing gross lesions or size changes must be examined. Skin, liver, kidney, and target organs should be preserved.
