## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes two in vivo guinea pig methods for assessing the skin sensitisation (allergic contact dermatitis) potential of test chemicals: the Guinea Pig Maximisation Test (GPMT) of Magnusson and Kligman, which uses Freund's Complete Adjuvant (FCA) to potentiate sensitisation, and the non-adjuvant Buehler Test. Animals are initially exposed to the test chemical during an induction phase, followed by a rest period of 10 to 14 days, and then a challenge dose is applied to compare the extent and degree of skin reaction in test animals versus sham-treated controls. For animal welfare reasons, these tests should only be conducted as a last resort when other in chemico, in vitro, or in vivo skin sensitisation test methods are not applicable.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- GPMT: Skin reactions graded at approximately 48 hours and 72 hours after start of challenge application using the Magnusson and Kligman scale (0 = no visible change; 1 = discrete or patchy erythema; 2 = moderate and confluent erythema; 3 = intense erythema and swelling)
- Buehler: Skin reactions graded at approximately 30 hours and 54 hours after application of the challenge patch using the same grading scale
- All skin reactions and unusual findings, including systemic reactions, from induction and challenge procedures observed and recorded
- Blind reading of test and control animals encouraged
- Body weights recorded at start and conclusion of test
- Rechallenge may be conducted approximately one week after first challenge to clarify equivocal results
- Reliability check using known mild-to-moderate sensitisers (e.g., hexyl cinnamic aldehyde, mercaptobenzothiazole, benzocaine) must have been performed no more than six months prior
