## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes an *in vivo* screening assay (Level 3 of the OECD Conceptual Framework) to identify substances with potential oestrogenic activity (agonists or antagonists). It evaluates the ability of a chemical to elicit biological activities consistent with agonists or antagonists of natural oestrogens by measuring uterine weight response in rodents. The assay is intended to be included in a battery of tests for endocrine disruptor assessment.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Daily observation for signs of toxicity, distress, or mortality.
- Monitoring of body weight (critical for immature model; less correlated in OVX adult model).
- Assessment of vaginal epithelial cells (for OVX adults to confirm absence of oestrus prior to use).
- Observation of oestrous cycle parameters (if applicable in immature models).

### Haematology
- Not a primary endpoint in this specific short-term screening guideline (focus is on organ weight and histopathology).

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Not a primary endpoint in this specific short-term screening guideline.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Excision and weighing of the uterus (wet weight).
- **Histopathology:** Examination of the vagina (keratinization/cornification), uterus, ovaries, and other relevant tissues to assess tissue growth vs. water imbibition and rule out non-oestrogenic effects.
- **Ovarian Stubs:** Examination in OVX animals to ensure complete removal of ovarian tissue.

### Footnotes
- **Uterus Weight:** The primary measure of oestrogenic activity. A statistically significant increase indicates a positive response.
- **Vaginal Cytology:** Used to verify the ovariectomised status (absence of oestrus) in adult females before dosing.
- **Phytoestrogens:** Diets must be low in phytoestrogens (<350 µg genistein equivalents/g for immature rats) to avoid confounding results.
