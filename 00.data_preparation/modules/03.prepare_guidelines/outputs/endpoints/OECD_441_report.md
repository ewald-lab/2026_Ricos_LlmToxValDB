## Purpose and Scope

The Hershberger Bioassay is a short-term *in vivo* screening test using accessory tissues of the male reproductive tract to evaluate the ability of a chemical to elicit biological activities consistent with androgen agonists, antagonists, or 5α-reductase inhibitors. It serves as a mechanistic assay within Level 3 of the OECD Conceptual Framework for Endocrine Disrupters, utilizing five specific androgen-dependent target tissues in castrated peripubertal male rats to detect changes in absolute weight.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations

- Monitoring for post-surgical complications following castration.
- Observation of body weight changes (used as a covariate if necessary).

### Clinical Biochemistry

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Excision and weighing of five specific androgen-dependent tissues approximately 24 hours after the last dose.
- **Target Tissues:** Ventral prostate (VP), Seminal vesicle (SV) plus fluids and coagulating glands, Levator ani-bulbocavernosus (LABC) muscle, Cowper's glands (COW), and Glans penis (GP).
- **Histopathology:** Not mandated as a primary endpoint in the core protocol for weight measurement, though tissue examination may be part of broader toxicological assessment.
