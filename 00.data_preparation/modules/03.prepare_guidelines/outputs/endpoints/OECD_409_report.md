## Purpose and Scope

This guideline provides information on the possible health hazards likely to arise from repeated exposure over a period of rapid growth and into young adulthood. The study provides data on major toxic effects, indicates target organs, assesses the possibility of accumulation, and helps estimate a no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) for selecting doses in chronic studies and establishing safety criteria for human exposure. It is intended for use when effects in other studies indicate a need for clarification in a second, non-rodent species, or when toxicokinetic studies justify a specific non-rodent choice.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- General clinical observations at least once daily.
- Detailed clinical observations at least once weekly (outside home cage).
- Signs recorded include skin/fur changes, eye/mucous membrane conditions, secretions/excretions, autonomic activity (lacrimation, piloerection), gait/posture changes, clonic/tonic movements, and bizarre behavior.
- Ophthalmological examination prior to dosing and at termination (all animals preferred; minimum high dose and control).

### Haematology
- Haematocrit
- Haemoglobin concentration
- Erythrocyte count
- Total and differential leukocyte count
- Platelet count
- Clotting potential (clotting time, prothrombin time, or thromboplastin time)
- Samples taken at start, midway/monthly, and end of study.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Electrolytes: Calcium, phosphorus, chloride, sodium, potassium
- Carbohydrate metabolism: Fasting glucose
- Liver function: Alanine aminotransferase (ALT), Aspartate aminotransferase (AST), Gamma glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT)
- Kidney function: Urea nitrogen, Blood creatinine
- Proteins: Albumin, Total serum protein
- Other: Total bilirubin, Ornithine decarboxylase
- Additional considerations: Lipids, hormones, acid/base balance, methaemoglobin, cholinesterase inhibition.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Full detailed examination of external surface, all orifices, and cranial/thoracic/abdominal cavities.
- **Organ Weights:** Liver, kidneys, adrenals, testes, epididymides, ovaries, uterus, thyroid (with parathyroids), thymus, spleen, brain, heart.
- **Histopathology:** Full histopathology on preserved organs/tissues for control and high-dose groups. Extended to other groups if treatment-related changes are observed.
- **Tissues Preserved:** Brain, spinal cord, pituitary, eyes, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, oesophagus, salivary glands, stomach, intestines, liver, gall bladder, pancreas, kidneys, adrenals, spleen, heart, trachea, lungs, aorta, gonads, uterus, accessory sex organs, mammary gland, prostate, urinary bladder, lymph nodes, peripheral nerve, bone marrow, skin.

### Footnotes

*   **Clinical Observations:** Effort should be made to ensure variations in observation conditions are minimal.
*   **Haematology/Clinical Biochemistry:** Animals should be fasted for a period appropriate to the species prior to blood sampling.
*   **Urinalysis:** Determinations should be performed using timed urine volume collection.
*   **Pathology:** Organs should be trimmed of adherent tissue and weighed wet as soon as possible after dissection.
*   **Histopathology:** Examination extended to all dosage groups if treatment-related changes are observed in the high dose group.
