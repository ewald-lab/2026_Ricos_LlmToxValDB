## Purpose and Scope

The 90-day study provides information on the possible health hazards likely to arise from repeated exposure over a prolonged period of time covering post-weaning maturation and growth into adulthood of the test animals. It provides information on major toxic effects, indicates target organs and the possibility of accumulation of test chemical, and can provide an estimate of a NOAEL of exposure. The revised guideline places additional emphasis on endocrine endpoints to combine with the existing sensitivity to neurological, immunological and reproductive effects.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- General clinical observations at least once daily, considering peak period of anticipated effects after dosing
- At least twice daily inspection of all animals for morbidity and mortality
- Detailed clinical observations at least once weekly outside the home cage in a standard arena
- Signs include changes in skin, fur, eyes, mucous membranes, secretions/excretions, autonomic activity (lacrimation, pilo-erection, pupil size, unusual respiratory pattern)
- Changes in gait, posture, response to handling, clonic or tonic movements, stereotypes, bizarre behaviour
- Ophthalmological examination prior to administration and at termination (at least high dose and control groups)
- Sensory reactivity assessment towards end of exposure period (not earlier than week 11): auditory, visual, proprioceptive stimuli
- Grip strength assessment towards end of exposure period
- Motor activity assessment towards end of exposure period
- Body weight measured at least once weekly
- Food consumption measured at least weekly; water consumption if test chemical administered via drinking water

### Haematology
- Haematocrit
- Haemoglobin concentration
- Erythrocyte count
- Reticulocyte count
- Total and differential leukocyte count
- Platelet count
- Measure of blood clotting time/potential

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Sodium, potassium, glucose, total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, urea, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, total protein, albumin
- More than two enzymes indicative of hepatocellular effects (e.g. alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, gamma glutamyl transpeptidase, sorbitol dehydrogenase)
- Bile acids and bilirubin as appropriate
- Calcium, phosphorus, fasting triglycerides, specific hormones, methaemoglobin, cholinesterase (case-by-case)
- Serum total T4, T3, and TSH (required) on samples from each animal at study termination
- Optional hormones: testosterone, oestradiol, FSH, LH (case-by-case)
- Optional urinalysis: appearance, volume, osmolality/specific gravity, pH, protein, glucose, blood/blood cells

### Pathology
- Full detailed gross necropsy of all animals (external surface, all orifices, cranial/thoracic/abdominal cavities)
- Mandatory organ weights: liver, kidneys, adrenals, testes, epididymides, prostate + seminal vesicles with coagulating glands, uterus, ovaries, thymus, spleen, brain, heart, pituitary gland, thyroid gland
- Tissue preservation for histopathology: all gross lesions, brain, spinal cord (cervical/mid-thoracic/lumbar), pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, oesophagus, salivary glands, stomach, small and large intestines (including Peyer's patches), liver, pancreas, kidneys, adrenals, spleen, heart, trachea and lungs, aorta, ovaries, uterus, cervix, vagina, testes, epididymides, prostate, seminal vesicles, coagulation glands, mammary gland (male and female), urinary bladder, gall bladder (mouse), lymph nodes, peripheral nerve, skeletal muscle, bone with bone marrow, skin, eyes
- Full histopathology on control and high dose groups; extended to other groups if treatment-related changes observed
- Oestrus cycle determination by vaginal smears at necropsy for all females
- Optional sperm assessments: cauda epididymis sperm reserves, sperm morphology, sperm motility
- Optional sample cryopreservation for omics investigations
