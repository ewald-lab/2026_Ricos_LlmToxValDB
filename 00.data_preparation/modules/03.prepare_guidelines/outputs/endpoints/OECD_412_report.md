## Purpose and Scope

OECD TG 412 is designed to fully characterize test chemical toxicity by the inhalation route over a subacute 28-day exposure period, providing robust data for quantitative inhalation risk assessments. Subacute inhalation toxicity studies are primarily used to derive regulatory concentrations for assessing worker risk in occupational settings as well as residential, consumer, transportation, and environmental risk. The guideline was revised in 2018 to accommodate testing of nanomaterials and to require mandatory bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) analysis for all test chemicals, with lung splitting for histopathology (left lung) and BAL (right lung).

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Animals clinically observed before, during, and after each daily exposure period, and during any post-exposure observation (PEO) periods
- Cage-side observations: changes in skin and fur, eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory and circulatory systems, nervous system, somatomotor activity and behaviour patterns
- Particular attention to tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, and coma
- Rectal temperature measurements may provide supportive evidence of reflex bradypnea, Paintal reflex, or hypo/hyperthermia
- Body weights recorded before first exposure (day 0), twice weekly thereafter, and at death or euthanasia; weekly if no significant effects in first 2 weeks
- Food consumption measured weekly; water consumption may also be measured
- Ophthalmological examination of fundus, refractive media, iris, and conjunctivae for all animals prior to treatment and for all high-concentration and control groups at termination

### Haematology
- Erythrocyte count, haematocrit, haemoglobin concentration
- Mean corpuscular haemoglobin, mean corpuscular volume, mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration
- Reticulocytes
- Total leukocyte count, differential leukocyte count, platelet count
- Clotting potential (select one): prothrombin time, clotting time, or partial thromboplastin time

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Glucose (with fasting considerations), total cholesterol, triglycerides, blood urea nitrogen, total bilirubin, creatinine
- Total protein, albumin, globulin
- Alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase
- Electrolytes: potassium, sodium, calcium, phosphorus, chloride
- Optional urinalysis: appearance (colour and turbidity), volume, specific gravity or osmolality, pH, total protein, glucose, blood/blood cells
- Additional optional parameters (at study director's discretion): cholinesterase, lipids, hormones, acid/base balance, methaemoglobin or Heinz bodies, creatine kinase, bone marrow cytology, troponins, lactate dehydrogenase, glutamate dehydrogenase, gamma glutamyl transpeptidase

### Pathology
- Complete gross necropsy for all test animals including those dying during the study; all gross pathological changes recorded with particular attention to the respiratory tract
- Organs and tissues preserved for histopathology (Table 2): adrenals, bone marrow, brain (cerebrum, cerebellum, medulla/pons), [eyes and eyelids], heart, kidneys, larynx (3 levels including base of epiglottis), liver, lung (left lung for histopathology including main bronchi and pleura), lymph nodes (hilar region and distal from portal-of-entry), nasopharyngeal tissues (at least 4 levels including nasopharyngeal duct and NALT), oesophagus, olfactory bulb, ovaries, seminal vesicles, spinal cord (cervical, mid-thoracic, lumbar), spleen, stomach, testes, thymus, thyroid, trachea (at least 2 levels), urinary bladder, uterus, all gross lesions
- Organs weighed wet as soon as possible after dissection (bold in Table 2): adrenals, brain, heart, kidneys, liver, lung (left lung), ovaries, spleen, testes, thymus, thyroid, uterus
- Histopathology performed on all Table 2 tissues for control and high-concentration groups; extended to other groups as needed for concentration-response
- Mandatory bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) analysis: lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), total protein or albumin, cell counts and differentials (alveolar macrophages, lymphocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils)
- Lung burden measurements when range-finding suggests poorly soluble particles retained in lung (Option B study design); performed in 5 males per concentration at PEO-1 (exposure termination) with optional PEO-2 and PEO-3 for clearance kinetics
- Pulmonary function and body temperature measurements when evidence of reflex bradypnea or Paintal reflex observed
