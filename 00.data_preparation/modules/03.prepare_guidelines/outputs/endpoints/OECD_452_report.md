## Purpose and Scope

This guideline provides information on the possible health hazards likely to arise from repeated exposure over a considerable part of the lifespan of the species used. It identifies chronic toxicity, target organs, dose-response relationships, and NOAELs/BMDs for establishing safety criteria. The study is primarily designed for rodents but principles apply to non-rodents with appropriate modifications.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
*   Morbidity/mortality checks (at least twice daily).
*   Detailed clinical observations (skin, fur, eyes, mucous membranes, secretions, autonomic activity, gait, posture, response to handling, clonic/tonic movements, stereotypies) at least once prior to dosing, end of first week, and monthly thereafter.
*   Ophthalmological examination prior to first administration and preferably at termination (at least high dose and control groups).
*   Neurotoxicity specific observations (sensory reactivity) if indicated by previous studies.

### Haematology
*   Full haematological examinations (specific parameters listed in detailed endpoints below) required for all groups.
*   Additional animals may be needed for mice to conduct all required haematological determinations.

### Clinical Biochemistry
*   Standard clinical biochemistry measurements (liver, kidney function, electrolytes, etc.) as part of the standard protocol.
*   Specific parameters detailed in the endpoint extraction section.

### Pathology
*   **Gross Necropsy:** All animals dying during the test and surviving animals at conclusion must be necropsied.
*   **Organ Weights:** Absolute and relative weights of specific organs (e.g., brain, liver, kidneys, adrenals, gonads, thyroid, spleen, heart, pituitary, prostate, seminal vesicles, thymus) are required.
*   **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of tissues from control and high-dose groups, and any other groups showing treatment-related changes. Tissues include brain, spinal cord, eyes, salivary glands, stomach, intestines, liver, kidneys, adrenals, gonads, thyroid, parathyroid, pancreas, spleen, thymus, lymph nodes, heart, lungs, urinary bladder, prostate/seminal vesicles, uterus/vagina, skin, bone marrow, and injection sites.
