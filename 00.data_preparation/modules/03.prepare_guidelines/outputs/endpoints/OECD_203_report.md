## Purpose and Scope

This test guideline describes an acute toxicity test to determine the concentration of a test chemical in water that kills 50% of the exposed fish (LC50). Fish are exposed to the test chemical for 96 hours under static, semi-static, or flow-through conditions. Mortalities and visible abnormalities related to appearance and behaviour are recorded. Before carrying out this test, consideration should be given to whether reliable information could be derived from alternative methods (QSAR, read-across, fish embryos, fish cell lines) in a weight-of-evidence approach.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Minimum 2 observations within first 24 hours with at least 3 hours between observations (e.g., at 2h, 5h, and 24h)
- Days 2-4: all vessels inspected twice per day (early morning and late afternoon)
- Mortalities recorded; dead fish removed as soon as observed (no visible movement, no reaction to touching caudal peduncle)
- Visible abnormalities recorded: loss of equilibrium (loss of balance, head up or down, floating at surface or sinking)
- Appearance changes: weak or dark pigmentation, exophthalmia
- Ventilatory behaviour: hyper, hypo, or irregular ventilation; coughing
- Swimming behaviour: hyper or hypo activity, immobility, convulsions, near surface or bottom, dense or loss of schooling
- Additional sublethal clinical signs may be recorded voluntarily (comprehensive list in Annex 4)
- Individual fish size (wet weight and total length) measured in a subsample of at least 10 fish prior to exposure
- Sample cryopreservation for omics investigations is optional (plasma and excess tissues or whole organism at 96 hrs)

### Haematology
- Not required in this guideline

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Not required in this guideline

### Pathology
- Not required in this guideline (surviving treatment fish euthanised at end of exposure; no necropsy/histopathology specified)

This guideline does not contain extractable endpoints. It is an acute ecotoxicology test focused on determining the LC50 (median lethal concentration) from mortality data. The guideline does not include organ weights, histopathology, clinical biochemistry, haematology, or hormone measurements.
