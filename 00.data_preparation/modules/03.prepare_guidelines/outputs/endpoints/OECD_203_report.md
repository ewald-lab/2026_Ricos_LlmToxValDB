## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes procedures for determining the acute toxicity of chemicals to fish. The test involves exposing fish to the test chemical for a period of 96 hours under controlled conditions to determine the median lethal concentration (LC50). It emphasizes animal welfare by encouraging alternative methods (QSAR, read-across, fish embryos) where possible and includes updated guidance on sublethal clinical signs and water quality parameters.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Mortality (no visible movement, no reaction to touch).
- Visible abnormalities regarding equilibrium (loss of balance, floating, sinking).
- Appearance changes (pigmentation, exophthalmia).
- Ventilatory behavior (hyper/hypo ventilation, coughing).
- Swimming behavior (immobility, convulsions, schooling loss).
- *Optional:* Additional sublethal clinical signs listed in Annex 4.

### Haematology
- Not applicable for this acute aquatic toxicity study.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Not applicable for this acute aquatic toxicity study.

### Pathology
- Humane killing of surviving treatment fish at the end of exposure.
- Sample cryopreservation (plasma/tissues) is optional for further investigations (e.g., omics).
- Measurement of individual size (wet weight and total length) prior to exposure.

### Footnotes

*   **Mortality:** Fish are considered dead if there is no visible movement (e.g., gill movements) and if touching of the caudal peduncle produces no reaction.
*   **Sublethal signs:** Reporting signs in Annex 4 is voluntary, while those in paragraphs 27-31 are mandatory.
*   **Chemical Analysis:** If concentrations decline by more than 20%, all concentrations should be measured. Ideally, maintained at ≥80% of nominal.
*   **Environmental:** For semi-static systems, DO, pH, salinity, and temperature measured prior to and after water renewal.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- **Mandatory Reporting:** Cumulative mortality at each concentration and time point.
- **Recommended Sublethal Signs:** Systematic recording of visible abnormalities to identify humane endpoints and mode of action. Key categories include:
  - Loss of equilibrium (horizontal/vertical orientation, buoyancy control).
  - Abnormal swimming (hypoactivity, hyperactivity, corkscrew, convulsions, tetany).
  - Respiratory distress (hyperventilation, hypoventilation, coughing, gulping).
  - Skin/Pigment changes (darkening, lightening, mottling, exophthalmia, oedema, haemorrhage).
  - Behavioral changes (schooling loss, aggression, irritation).
- **Note:** While the major categories are "mandatory" for the purpose of defining moribundity in the context of the test, the detailed sub-categories in Annex 4 Table 1 are encouraged for voluntary systematic recording to improve data quality and future consensus.

### Haematology
- No haematological examinations are required for this acute toxicity test.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- No clinical biochemistry determinations are required for this acute toxicity test.

### Pathology
- No gross necropsy or histopathology is mandated by the standard protocol for LC50 determination, though visible abnormalities (gross pathology) are recorded as clinical signs.
