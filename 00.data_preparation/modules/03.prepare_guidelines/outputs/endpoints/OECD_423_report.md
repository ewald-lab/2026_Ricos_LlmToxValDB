## Purpose and Scope

OECD 423 describes a stepwise acute toxic class method for classifying the acute oral toxicity of test substances according to the GHS (Globally Harmonised System) categories. The method uses 3 animals of a single sex per step and, based on mortality and/or moribund status, determines which of a series of fixed LD50 cut-off ranges (categories 1–5) the substance falls into. It is an animal-welfare-conscious alternative to the classical LD50 test, using very few animals while providing reproducible classification results.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Animals observed individually at least once during the first 30 minutes after dosing
- Periodic observations during the first 24 hours, with special attention during the first 4 hours
- Daily observations thereafter for the full 14-day observation period (may be extended if needed)
- Observations cover: changes in skin and fur, eyes and mucous membranes, respiratory, circulatory, autonomic and central nervous systems, somatomotor activity, and behaviour pattern
- Specific attention to tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, and coma
- Times of onset and disappearance of signs of toxicity recorded; delayed toxicity noted
- Moribund animals or those in severe distress humanely killed; time of death recorded as precisely as possible
- Animals fasted prior to dosing (rat: overnight fast with water; mouse: 3–4 hour fast with water)
- Body weight determined shortly before dosing and at least weekly thereafter; terminal weight at study end

### Haematology
- Not required in this guideline

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Not required in this guideline

### Pathology
- All test animals (including those dying during the test or removed for welfare reasons) subjected to gross necropsy
- All gross pathological changes recorded for each animal
- Microscopic examination of organs showing evidence of gross pathology in animals surviving 24 or more hours may be considered

This guideline does not contain extractable endpoints. It is an acute oral toxicity classification test focused on assigning GHS hazard categories using fixed doses (5, 50, 300, 2000 mg/kg) in a stepwise procedure. The guideline does not include organ weights, haematology panels, clinical biochemistry panels, histopathology endpoint lists, or hormone measurements. Observations are limited to general clinical signs, body weight, mortality/morbidity, gross necropsy findings, and optional microscopic examination of organs with gross pathology.
