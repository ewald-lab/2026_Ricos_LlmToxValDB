## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes the "Acute Toxic Class Method," a stepwise procedure for determining the acute oral toxicity of substances. It uses a minimum number of animals (typically 3 per step) to classify substances into hazard categories based on fixed LD50 cut-off values defined by the Globally Harmonised System (GHS). The method is an alternative to the conventional LD50 test (TG 401), aiming to reduce animal use while providing sufficient information for classification and labeling. It does not aim to calculate a precise LD50 value but rather to rank substances into toxicity classes.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
*   Individual observations at least once during the first 30 minutes after dosing.
*   Periodic observations during the first 24 hours, with special attention during the first 4 hours.
*   Daily observations thereafter for a total of 14 days.
*   Specific signs to monitor: changes in skin/fur, eyes/mucous membranes, respiratory/circulatory/CNS function, somatomotor activity, behavior patterns.
*   Specific toxic signs: tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, coma.
*   Time of onset and disappearance of toxic signs must be recorded.
*   Moribund or severely distressed animals should be humanely killed; time of death recorded.

### Haematology
*   *Not applicable.* This is an acute toxicity test focused on mortality and gross pathology; haematology is not a standard requirement unless specific regulatory needs dictate otherwise (not mentioned in the standard protocol).

### Clinical Biochemistry
*   *Not applicable.* Not a standard requirement for this acute toxicity guideline.

### Pathology
*   **Gross Necropsy:** All test animals (including those that die during the test or are removed for welfare reasons) must undergo gross necropsy.
*   **Microscopic Examination:** Considered optional/useful for organs showing evidence of gross pathology in animals surviving 24 or more hours.
*   **Recording:** All gross pathological changes must be recorded for each animal.
