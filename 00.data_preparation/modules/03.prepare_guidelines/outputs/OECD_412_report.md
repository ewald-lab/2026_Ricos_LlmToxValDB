## Purpose and Scope

This revised Test Guideline 412 is designed to fully characterize test chemical toxicity by the inhalation route for a subacute duration (28 days), providing robust data for quantitative inhalation risk assessments. It accommodates testing of nanomaterials and reflects evolving science for gases, vapours, and aerosols, requiring specific measurements such as bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) analysis and lung burden measurements when appropriate.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
*   Daily clinical observations before, during, and after exposure periods.
*   Specific attention to skin/fur, eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory/circulatory systems, nervous system, and somatomotor activity.
*   Monitoring for tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, and coma.
*   Rectal temperature measurement may be performed.

### Haematology
*   Erythrocyte count
*   Haematocrit

*   Mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration

*   Clotting potential (Prothrombin time, Clotting time, or Partial thromboplastin time)

*   Urinalysis parameters (optional): Appearance, Volume, Specific gravity/osmolality, pH, Total protein, Glucose, Blood/blood cells

### Pathology
*   **Gross Necropsy:** Complete examination of all animals, including dead ones; particular attention to respiratory tract changes.
*   **Organ Weights:** Wet weights of specified organs (Adrenals, Brain, Heart, Kidneys, Liver, Lungs, Spleen, Testes, Ovaries, Uterus, Thyroid, Thymus, etc.).
*   **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of preserved tissues from control and high-dose groups, plus any animals dying during the study. Includes nasal cavity, larynx, trachea, lungs, lymph nodes, and systemic organs.
*   **Bronchoalveolar Lavage Fluid (BALF):** Cell counts and differentials (macrophages, lymphocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils), LDH, total protein/albumin.
*   **Lung Burden:** Measurement of retained particles in lungs (mandatory for poorly soluble solid aerosols).

### Footnotes

*   *Glucose*: Because a lengthy fasting period can introduce bias, the study director should determine whether it is appropriate to fast the animals. If fasting is used, it should be appropriate to the species (e.g., 16 h overnight for rats).
*   *Urinalysis*: Not required on a routine basis, but may be performed when deemed useful based on expected or observed toxicity.
*   *Lung Burden*: Males are used because they have a higher minute volume than females. Measurements are typically done at PEO-1 (within 24h of exposure termination). Additional PEOs may be added for clearance kinetics.
*   *Histopathology*: Particular attention should be paid to the respiratory tract (nasal cavity, larynx, trachea, lungs) and target organs.
