## Purpose and Scope

This guideline is designed to fully characterize test chemical toxicity by the inhalation route for a subacute duration (28 days) and provide robust data for quantitative inhalation risk assessments. It specifically accommodates testing of nanomaterials, gases, vapours, and aerosols, addressing study design dependencies on the physical form of the chemical and lung retention characteristics. The primary impetus for this revision was to accommodate nanomaterials and reflect evolving state-of-the-science.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- General clinical signs (morbidity, mortality, pain, distress) observed daily.
- Body weight measurements at start, weekly, and at termination.
- Food consumption (if applicable, though often withheld during exposure).
- Water consumption (for whole-body exposure).
- Ophthalmological examination (optional/recommended if systemic effects suspected).
- Pulmonary function tests (optional, e.g., in range-finding).

### Haematology
- Erythrocyte count, haemoglobin concentration, haematocrit.
- Total leukocyte count and differential leukocyte count.
- Platelet count.
- Prothrombin time (or other coagulation parameters if indicated).
- *Note: Specific parameters depend on the extent of systemic toxicity evaluation.*

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Liver enzymes: ALT, AST, ALP, GGT.
- Kidney function: Creatinine, Urea Nitrogen (BUN).
- Electrolytes: Sodium, Potassium, Chloride, Calcium.
- Metabolic markers: Glucose, Cholesterol, Total Protein, Albumin, Bilirubin.
- *Note: Additional parameters may be selected based on expected systemic toxicity (e.g., immune, neurologic).*

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Examination of all major organs and tissues.
- **Organ Weights:** Absolute and relative weights of specific organs (see Endpoints table).
- **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of tissues, with mandatory focus on respiratory tract (nasal cavity, larynx, trachea, lungs).
- **Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BALF):** Mandatory for all test chemicals. Analysis includes cell counts (total and differential), protein, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), glucose, alkaline phosphatase, and cytology.
- **Lung Burden:** Required for poorly soluble particles likely to be retained (Option B). Includes measurement of test substance in lung and lymph nodes.

### Footnotes

- **BALF Splitting:** The left lung is reserved for histopathology, while the right lung is used for BALF analysis.
- **Lung Burden:** Mandatory only if the test chemical is a poorly soluble solid aerosol likely to be retained (Option B).
- **Recovery Groups:** Satellite groups (PEO-2) are required for recovery assessment; males in these groups may be used for lung burden if Option B is active, leaving females for BALF.
- **Particle Size:** MMAD should ideally be ≤2 µm with GSD 1-3 for nano-range aerosols.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Daily monitoring for signs of toxicity, mortality, nature, severity, time of onset, and duration of effects.
- Detailed tabulation of individual animal responses.

### Haematology & Clinical Pathology
- Full clinical pathology data tabulation (standard haematology and clinical biochemistry panels implied by "clinical pathology").
- Specific focus on parameters relevant to inhalation toxicity.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Included within the "Clinical pathology" requirement.

### Pathology
- **Gross Pathology**: Examination of all organs.
- **Organ Weights**: Specifically **Left Lung** weight is mandatory in the main study.
- **Histopathology**: **Left Lung** histopathology is mandatory in the main study.
- **Specialized Analyses**: 
  - Bronchoalveolar Lavage Fluid (BALF) analysis (Right Lung).
  - Lung burden determination (mandatory for poorly soluble solids; conditional for others).
  - Pulmonary function tests and body temperature (when indicated).
- **Systemic Assessments**: Optional evaluations for immunotoxicity, neurotoxicity, hepatotoxicity, and cardiovascular effects.
