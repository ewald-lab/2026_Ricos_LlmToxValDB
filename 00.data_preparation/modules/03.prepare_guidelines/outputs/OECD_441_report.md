## Purpose and Scope

The Hershberger Bioassay is a short-term in vivo screening test using accessory tissues of the male reproductive tract in castrated peripubertal male rats. It evaluates the ability of a chemical to elicit biological activities consistent with androgen agonists, antagonists, or 5α-reductase inhibitors by measuring changes in the absolute weight of five specific androgen-dependent tissues. The assay serves as a mechanistic screening tool within Level 3 of the OECD Conceptual Framework for Endocrine Disrupters.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Excision and weighing of five specific target tissues:
  - Ventral prostate (VP)
  - Seminal vesicle (SV) (including fluids and coagulating glands)
  - Levator ani-bulbocavernosus (LABC) muscle
  - Paired Cowper's glands (COW)
  - Glans penis (GP)
- **Histopathology:** Not explicitly mandated as a routine requirement for all groups in the provided text, though tissue condition is assessed via weight.

### Footnotes

*   **Body Weight:** Variations should not exceed ±20% of mean weight at study commencement. Used as a statistical covariate.
*   **Glans Penis:** Can only be measured after prepuce separation; strains maturing later than 42 days (e.g., Fisher 344) require special consideration or older castration age.
*   **CV Limits:** Performance criteria based on Coefficient of Variation (CV) for each tissue (e.g., SV max 40%, VP max 40-45%).
