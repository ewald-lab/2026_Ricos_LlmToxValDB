## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes a procedure for assessing the acute dermal toxicity of chemicals using a stepwise fixed-dose approach. It is designed to classify chemicals according to the Globally Harmonised System (GHS) while minimizing animal use by testing single animals sequentially or in small groups (up to 2) at specific dose levels. The method focuses on observing mortality and signs of toxicity over a 14-day period to determine hazard classification without necessarily calculating an exact LD50 value.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Immediate observation within 30 minutes post-dosing.
- Periodic observations during the first 24 hours (especially 2–6 hours).
- Daily observations thereafter for up to 14 days.
- Specific attention to: changes in skin/fur, eyes/mucous membranes, respiratory/circulatory/CNS effects, somatomotor activity, tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, and coma.
- Assessment of the treatment site using Draize criteria at 24, 48, and 72 hours post-removal.

### Haematology
- *Not required* for this acute study protocol.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- *Not required* for this acute study protocol.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** Mandatory for all animals (including those dying during the test or killed for humane reasons). All gross pathological changes must be recorded.
- **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination is optional but recommended for organs showing gross pathology or the treatment site in animals surviving 24+ hours after dosing.
