## Purpose and Scope

This guideline provides information on the possible health hazards likely to arise from repeated oral exposure over a prolonged period (90 days), covering post-weaning maturation into adulthood. It aims to identify major toxic effects, indicate target organs, assess accumulation potential, and estimate a No-Observed-Adverse-Effect Level (NOAEL). The study is updated to include specific endocrine-sensitive endpoints (thyroid hormones and lipids) to improve the detection of potential endocrine activity, alongside neurological, immunological, and reproductive effects.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
*   General clinical observations made at least once daily (morbidity/mortality signs twice daily).
*   Detailed clinical observations (outside home cage) at least once prior to dosing and weekly thereafter.
*   Signs recorded: skin/fur changes, eyes, mucous membranes, secretions/excretions, autonomic activity (lacrimation, pilo-erection), gait/posture, clonic/tonic movements, stereotypes, bizarre behavior.
*   Ophthalmological examination prior to dosing and at termination (all animals preferred; minimum high dose and control).
*   Functional observations towards the end of the study (sensory reactivity, grip strength, motor activity).

### Haematology
*   Haematocrit
*   Haemoglobin concentration
*   Erythrocyte count
*   Reticulocyte count
*   Total leukocyte count
*   Differential leukocyte count
*   Platelet count
*   Blood clotting time/potential measure

### Clinical Biochemistry
*   Electrolytes: Sodium, Potassium
*   Metabolic markers: Glucose, Urea, Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN), Creatinine
*   Proteins: Total protein, Albumin
*   Lipids: Total cholesterol, HDL, LDL
*   Liver enzymes: ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, Sorbitol dehydrogenase (at least two indicative of hepatocellular effects)
*   Optional/Conditional: Calcium, Phosphorus, Fasting triglycerides, Bilirubin, Bile acids, Methaemoglobin, Cholinesterase
*   **Required Hormones:** Serum T4, T3, TSH (measured in all main/satellite groups at termination)
*   **Optional Hormones:** Testosterone, Oestradiol, FSH, LH (case-by-case basis)

### Pathology
*   **Gross Necropsy:** Full detailed examination of external surface, orifices, cranial/thoracic/abdominal cavities.
*   **Organ Weights (Required):** Liver, Kidneys, Adrenals, Testes, Epididymides, Prostate + Seminal Vesicles + Coagulating glands (combined or dissected), Uterus, Ovaries, Thymus, Spleen, Brain, Heart, Thyroid (with extreme care). Pituitary (fresh or post-fixation).
*   **Histopathology (Required for Control & High Dose):** All preserved tissues including brain, spinal cord, pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, oesophagus, salivary glands, stomach, intestines, liver, pancreas, kidneys, adrenals, spleen, heart, trachea, lungs, aorta, ovaries, uterus, cervix, vagina, testes, epididymides, prostate, seminal vesicles, coagulation glands, mammary gland, urinary bladder, lymph nodes, peripheral nerve, skeletal muscle, bone/marrow, skin, eyes (if lesions observed).
*   **Sperm Measures (Optional):** Cauda epididymis sperm reserves, sperm morphology, sperm motility (can be restricted to control/high dose unless effects observed).
*   **Urinalysis (Optional):** Appearance, volume, osmolality/specific gravity, pH, protein, glucose, blood/blood cells.

### Footnotes

*   *Footnote 1:* For a number of measurements in serum and plasma, most notably for glucose, overnight fasting would be preferable. The major reason for this preference is that the increased variability which would inevitably result from non-fasting, would tend to mask more subtle effects and make interpretation difficult. On the other hand, however, overnight fasting may interfere with the general metabolism of the animals and, particularly in feeding studies, may disturb the daily exposure to the test chemical. If overnight fasting is adopted, clinical biochemical determinations should be performed after the conduct of functional observations of the study.
*   *Footnote 2:* The condition of the oestrogen-sensitive organs in the female should be assessed with reference to the stage of oestrus cycle at termination.
*   *Footnote 3:* Recommended procedures for preservation for omics are available in the OECD Guidance document No. 409.
