## Purpose and Scope

This guideline is designed to obtain sufficient information on the acute toxicity of a test article to enable its classification and provide lethality data (e.g., LC50, LC01, and slope) for one or both sexes as needed for quantitative risk assessments. It features two study types: a Traditional LC50 protocol and a Concentration x Time (C x t) protocol. The guideline allows for ranking and classification according to the UN GHS system.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Continuous monitoring for mortality and morbidity during exposure.
- Daily clinical observations for at least 14 days post-exposure (or until death).
- Recording of specific signs of toxicity (e.g., changes in skin/fur, eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory effects, convulsions, tremors, salivation, diarrhea, lethargy, sleep, coma).
- Assessment of sex susceptibility.

### Haematology
- *Not required.* (Acute inhalation studies generally do not include haematology unless specifically justified for regulatory needs beyond standard classification).

### Clinical Biochemistry
- *Not required.* (Standard protocol focuses on mortality and gross pathology).

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** All animals that die during the study and those surviving to termination shall undergo a gross necropsy.
- **Observations:** Examination of all external surfaces, all orifices, and the cranial, thoracic, and abdominal cavities and their contents.
- **Tissue Preservation:** Any tissue showing gross lesions should be preserved.
