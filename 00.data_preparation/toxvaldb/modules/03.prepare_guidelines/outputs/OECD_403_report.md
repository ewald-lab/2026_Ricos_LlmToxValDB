## Purpose and Scope

This revised Test Guideline 403 is designed to provide flexible acute inhalation toxicity data to enable classification according to the UN Globally Harmonized System (GHS) and quantitative risk assessment. It offers two protocols: a Traditional LC50 protocol and a Concentration x Time (C x t) protocol, allowing derivation of median lethal concentration (LC50), non-lethal thresholds (e.g., LC01), and slope values while identifying potential sex susceptibility.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
*   Frequent observations during exposure.
*   At least twice on the day of exposure, then at least once daily for 14 days (or until recovery/signs resolve).
*   Monitor skin/fur, eyes/mucous membranes, respiratory/circulatory/autonomic/CNS systems, somatomotor activity, behavior.
*   Specific attention to tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, coma.
*   Rectal temperature measurement may be recorded.
*   Record time of onset and disappearance of signs.

### Haematology
*   *Not explicitly required in standard TG 403 body text.* (Note: TG 403 focuses on acute lethality and gross pathology; haematology is not a mandatory endpoint in this specific guideline compared to sub-chronic guidelines like TG 407/408).

### Clinical Biochemistry
*   *Not explicitly required in standard TG 403 body text.* (Similar to haematology, clinical biochemistry is not a standard mandatory endpoint for acute inhalation lethality studies under TG 403 unless specifically justified for mechanistic insight, which is rare in the core protocol).

### Pathology
*   **Gross Necropsy:** All animals (including those dying during test or euthanized for welfare) must undergo gross necropsy.
*   **Respiratory Tract:** Particular attention to changes in the respiratory tract.
*   **Lung Weight:** Measuring lung weight of surviving rats is an additional examination that may be considered a priori to extend interpretive value.
*   **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of the respiratory tract may be included a priori, particularly for reactive articles (acids, hygroscopic materials) or if gross pathology indicates irritation. Other organs showing gross pathology or known to be affected should be examined microscopically.
