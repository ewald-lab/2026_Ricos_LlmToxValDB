## Purpose and Scope

This guideline describes the Acute Toxic Class Method for determining the acute oral toxicity of chemicals. It is a stepwise procedure designed to classify substances into GHS hazard categories based on mortality outcomes at fixed dose levels, using significantly fewer animals than conventional LD50 tests. The method allows for ranking substances for classification and hazard assessment purposes rather than calculating a precise LD50 value.

## Key Observations

### Clinical Observations
- Animals observed individually at least once during the first 30 minutes after dosing.
- Periodic observations during the first 24 hours, with special attention in the first 4 hours.
- Daily observations thereafter for a total of 14 days (or until recovery/death).
- Observations include changes in skin and fur, eyes and mucous membranes, respiratory, circulatory, autonomic and central nervous systems, somatomotor activity, and behaviour pattern.
- Specific attention to tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep, and coma.
- Signs of toxicity recorded with time course of onset and reversibility.

### Haematology
- Not required for this acute toxicity guideline.

### Clinical Biochemistry
- Not required for this acute toxicity guideline.

### Pathology
- **Gross Necropsy:** All test animals (including those dying during the test or humanely killed) must undergo gross necropsy. All gross pathological changes recorded for each animal.
- **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of organs showing evidence of gross pathology in animals surviving 24 or more hours may be considered if it yields useful information.
