This ontology follows the Human Disease Ontology (DOID), developed by the Disease Ontology group as a standardized vocabulary of human diseases, organised under the root term "disease" by anatomical location, infectious agent, cellular proliferation, metabolism, genetics and mental health. It should be used to classify findings according to the named disease or disorder they describe, rather than a single symptom, measurement or histopathological lesion. More info at https://disease-ontology.org/ Since this ontology is centred on named human diseases, we expect that dose-response measurements, animal-specific or mechanistic findings and isolated signs may not have a direct match.
